# Signal Separation Based on Fractional-Order Fourier Transform for Overlapping Linear Frequency Modulated Signals [with MATLAB Code]

Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM) excitation combined with pulse compression enhances the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the receiver. LFM signals have a longer duration than pulse signals of the same bandwidth. Therefore, in many practical scenarios, maintaining the time separation between echoes is impossible. In the case of analyzing individual LFM signals, separation techniques are required. Time-windowing cannot separate signals that overlap temporally. Frequency-domain filtering cannot separate signals with overlapping spectra. This paper introduces a method for separating time-overlapping LFM signals by applying Fractional Fourier Transform (FrFT). It briefly introduces FrFT, its operation, and computation. The proposed signal separation method is demonstrated by applying it to an analog ultrasound signal created by summing multiple time-overlapping LFM signals and component signals recovered with a ±0.6% spectral error. The experimental results show that the proposed separation method can be applied to time-overlapping LFM signals, creating them through transmission of LFM signals on stainless steel plates and water pipes.
Although LFM signals are broadband, their linear relationship between frequency and time allows them to be considered as narrowband at any given moment. If multiple LFM signals overlap in time and there is a temporal delay between each LFM signal, then the signal becomes unique and can be separated in instantaneous frequency.
This paper investigates the application of Fractional Fourier Transform (FrFT) in the separation of time-overlapping LFM signals in the time domain. In the second part, the basic characteristics of linear frequency modulation signals are introduced, and a synthetic ultrasound signal is created by superimposing multiple time-overlapping linear frequency modulation signals. Section three introduces FrFT, discrete time FrFT, and its digital computational methods. Section four proposes a method for separating overlapping LFM signals using FrFT. The synthetic signal created in Section II is used to demonstrate this separation method. In Section five, the separation method based on FrFT is applied to experimental data from industrial scenarios where overlapping signals exist.
This paper describes a time-frequency filtering technique based on fractional Fourier transform (FRFT) for separating overlapping LFM ultrasonic signals. Combining LFM excitation with pulse compression provides an increase in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) at the receiver, which is beneficial for detecting ultrasonic measurements with high signal attenuation. LFM signals have a longer duration than pulse signals, thus increasing the possibility of echoes overlapping in time. Time-domain overlapping LFM signals cannot be separated using conventional time-domain windowing or frequency-domain filtering due to their spectral overlap. FRFT allows spatial filtering in the time-frequency plane, thereby overcoming the limitations of filtering in either the time domain or frequency domain. The transformation of time-overlapping LFM signals with the optimal order of FRFT, such as defined by the chirp rate of the LFM excitation, maximizes the compression of the signal in the fractional domain. The time-overlapping signals become separated, and windows are applied to isolate each signal. Depending on the application, these signals can be converted to the time domain or frequency domain for further analysis. This technique has been proven to use simulated time-overlapping LFM signals. By comparing the spectrum of the source signal with the spectrum of the FRFT-separated signal, the maximum error is ±0.6%.
This technology is particularly suitable for analyzing ultrasonic signals generated when encountering layered structures, leading to time-overlapping echoes. Therefore, two experimental studies have shown that successful separation of time-overlapping echoes from stainless steel plate submerged tests and non-invasive transmission through water-filled stainless steel tubes. Many practical industrial applications, such as composite material analysis, non-invasive flow measurement, multiphase flow imaging, and spectroscopy. This FRFT-based signal separation technology shows great potential because the ability to decompose time-overlapping signals will allow the development of new measurement applications.
